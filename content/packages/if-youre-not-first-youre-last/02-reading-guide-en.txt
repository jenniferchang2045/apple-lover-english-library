If You're Not First, You're Last
Original reading guide

Use this beside the PDF you already own. Write answers in your own words. Do not paste pages from the book into these notes.


1. IDEA CAST (LEARN THESE FIRST)

First vs last
  What: A position in the buyer's mind and in the order book, not a personality type.
  Why: The book treats "almost winning" as a warning light, not as a compliment.

Activity
  What: Countable actions (calls, meetings, proposals, follow-ups), not "being busy."
  Why: In a bad market, people cut the only thing that still produces chance.

Competitor
  What: Anyone the buyer could choose instead of you, including "do nothing."
  Why: If you cannot name who is taking the slot, you are not competing. You are hoping.

Downturn
  What: A period when buyers delay and sellers shrink.
  Why: The book says this is when weak players leave space. Space is only useful if you move into it.

Price panic
  What: Discounting to relieve your own fear.
  Why: Cheap can look like action while it trains the market to wait for cheaper.

Accountability
  What: A number you cannot lie about at the end of the day.
  Why: Motivation fades. A count does not.

Attitude as a tool
  What: Energy the customer can feel.
  Why: Cardone treats mood as part of the offer. You can reject the style and still ask: do I sound like someone who expects to lose?


2. HOW THE BOOK IS BUILT

Think of it as a field manual, not a memoir.

- Opening: why "second" is a dangerous story to tell yourself.
- Middle: what to do when the market tightens — more contact, more offers, more pressure on your own excuses.
- Close: habits that keep you from sliding back into "wait and see."

You do not need a perfect chapter map to study it. You need a loop: rule → my market → action → count.


3. SPINE WITHOUT COPYING THE PAGES

A chapter-by-chapter dump would still miss the book, and it would start to sound like the book. Here is an original paraphrase of the spine:

When money gets tight, most sellers reduce activity and call it realism. Cardone's claim is the reverse: the environment is a reason to raise useful activity and to take accounts from people who have already quit in practice. "First" means you occupy the position others must spend energy to attack. "Last" means you are easy to ignore, easy to delay, and easy to replace.

The practical work is unromantic. Name the market. Name the rival. Increase the number of real conversations. Stop using the news as a blanket. Stop using discounting as courage. Keep a scoreboard that would embarrass you if it stayed empty.

If you finish the PDF and cannot point to one changed weekly number, you read a speech. You did not study a manual.


4. THEMES TO TRACK (WRITE ONE EXAMPLE EACH)

1. Position, not vibes
   Where in your week does a buyer already think of someone else first?

2. Volume of the right actions
   Which action, if doubled, would change the month? Which action only soothes you?

3. Competitors as a map
   List three rivals, including "the customer waits." What do they do that you refuse to do?

4. Fear dressed up as strategy
   Where do you call hesitation "research," "branding," or "waiting for Q4"?

5. Price as a story
   When you cut price, what are you trying to stop feeling?

6. Identity after a win
   After one good week, do you protect the win or raise the quota?


5. ENGLISH-STUDY NOTES

These are original explanations, not extracts.

Pattern: imperative voice (do this, stop that)
  What to do: Rewrite the command as "If I am in situation X, I will do Y."

Pattern: binary slogans (first/last, win/lose)
  What to do: Add a third column: "good enough this week." Then decide if the slogan is still useful.

Pattern: sales jargon
  What to do: Keep a personal glossary. One line each: pipeline, appointment, objection, follow-up, market share, closer.

Pattern: anecdote → rule
  What to do: Ignore names and cars. Keep only the rule you can test.

Pattern: repetition
  What to do: The third time you see the same claim, write "already noted" and move on.

Useful word families to collect: dominate, competitor, downturn, quota, follow-up, appointment, objection, discount, urgency, accountability. Write your own example sentence for each. Do not copy sentences from the PDF.


6. STUDY QUESTIONS

Answer in 8-12 sentences of your own English.

1. In your work, what would "first" look like in 90 days? Be specific enough that a stranger could check.

2. Which of your current habits looks like "last" behavior dressed up as professionalism?

3. Name one competitor. What do they do more often than you? What do you do that they do not?

4. If your market got 30% harder next month, which activity would you cut first? Why is that the wrong cut?

5. Where does price panic show up in your last quarter? What would a non-panic response have been?

6. What daily number would make this book useful instead of loud?


7. A ONE-WEEK READING PLAN

Day 1
  This pack + your definition of "first." Skim the opening of the PDF. Do not highlight slogans; write one rule.

Days 2-3
  Middle of the book. Fill the three columns: activity / avoidance / competitor.

Days 4-5
  Finish the PDF. Write a one-page plan: five actions, five numbers, one rival.

Day 6
  Mind map. Add two branches that are about your job, not about the author's life.

Day 7
  Answer three study questions. Then open only your one-page plan. If the plan is vague, the reading failed.
